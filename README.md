A demand forecast provides a basis for load scheduling, but forecast errors can change whether a planned transfer reduces the daily peak. We examine this problem using the publicly available Power Grid Company of Bangladesh (PGCB) dataset. The Uncertainty-Aware Demand Water-Filling (UAD-WF) framework combines a Ridge forecaster, historical forecast-error margins, bounded hourly adjustments, and a deterministic peak-to-valley transfer rule. Comparisons use linear programming (LP) and mixed-integer linear programming (MILP), with independent hour selection and exact shifted-energy matching for the MILP references. Across 300 eligible days in 2024, Ridge yields a mean absolute error of 695.71 MW and a mean absolute percentage error of 7.21%. With 15% assumed hourly flexibility, UAD-WF lowers the simulated daily peak by 6.30% on average; the corresponding robust MILP reduction is 5.76%. The mean paired advantage is 0.54 percentage points, with an exploratory calendar-week bootstrap 95% interval of [0.38, 0.69]. Both methods meet the retrospective flexibility limits on 97.0% of days. SHAP identifies historical demand as the main influence on Ridge predictions. These results concern counterfactual peak management under assumed flexibility. They neither establish a new general-purpose optimization principle nor demonstrate physical energy savings, deployment feasibility, or actual avoided load shedding.
